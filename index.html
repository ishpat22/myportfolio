PROJECT 01 — DETAILED REPORT

Topology Optimized Carbon Fibre Brake Mount Design

SolidWorksANSYS StructuralCarbon Fiber ManufacturingDFM3D Printing

Problem Statement

The previous year’s brake mount design exhibited excessive mass and stress concentrations under a combined 343 N per-wheel load (derived from vehicle weight distribution and braking forces). Additionally, the geometry had to strictly adhere to the manufacturing constraints of mold-based carbon-fibre forging.

Objective

Design and optimize a lightweight carbon-fibre brake mount that reduces overall mass and lowers maximum von Mises stress compared to the legacy design, while ensuring full compatibility with 3D-printed carbon forging mold manufacturing processes.

Visual Design Workflow

The project followed a 5-step iterative workflow from legacy baseline to final validated component:

01

Initial Baseline CAD

Modeled baseline geometry in SolidWorks using researched low stress geometries to establish mounting interfaces and manufacturing constraints.

02

Baseline FEA Analysis

Simulated 343 N loading conditions in ANSYS, identifying severe stress concentrations and high mass.

03

Topology Optimization

Executed optimization algorithms to strip 50% of material from low-stress neutral axis regions.

04

Final Part Redesign

Refined optimized geometry for mold-based carbon-fibre forging and manufacturing constraints.

05

Final Validation

Verified a 50% mass reduction and 23% lower peak stress under identical load conditions.

PROJECT 02 — DETAILED REPORT

Pressure Vessel Optimization & FEA Analysis

ANSYS StructuralSolidWorks

Problem Statement

Conventional pressure vessel design often relies on non-optimized wall thicknesses that introduce excessive structural weight or localized stress failures under operating pressures. The challenge lies in systematically evaluating candidate materials and fine-tuning vessel geometry to balance structural safety and weight efficiency.

Objective & Optimization Methodology

Evaluate structural performance across varying wall thicknesses using ANSYS static structural analysis and mesh convergence verification. To systematically determine the optimal balance between weight savings and structural compliance, we employed a Mass-Strain Product Optimization Framework. By multiplying the total component mass by the peak equivalent elastic strain, this metric penalizes designs that are excessively heavy (high mass) as well as those operating too close to their deformation limits (high strain). Minimizing this combined product isolates the true global optimum where structural rigidity and weight efficiency intersect.

Visual Design Workflow

The project followed a structured 5-step engineering process from initial load setup to final validated stress-strain performance and dimensions:

01

Loads & Boundary Conditions

Applied 15.1685 MPa internal pressure and fixed supports at the cylinder base and top valve neck.

02

Mesh Convergence Verification

Conducted manual refinement on the high-stress inner face until maximum stress variance flattened to 0.0097%.

03

Preliminary Material Optimization

Evaluated mass-strain product trajectories for Aluminum 6061, 304 Stainless Steel, and AISI 4130 Steel across 8–12 mm integer steps, identifying AISI 4130 as yielding the lowest minimum. Click image to compare all 3 materials ↗

04

Reiterated Optimization

Performed high-resolution 0.1 mm step fine-tuning on AISI 4130 Steel to isolate the true global minimum.

Final Validation & Dimensions

Established the final optimal 11.4 mm wall thickness, achieving a 13% reduction in max stress and an 11% reduction in max strain compared to original baseline dimensions, validating stress and strain distributions against yield safety limits.

PROJECT 03 — DETAILED REPORT

Microstructural Characterization & Crystallography

Materials CharacterizationMicroscopyPython

Scope & Overview

This portfolio showcase summarizes three core laboratory investigations completed over the semester for MSE219 (Structure and Characterization of Materials). The coursework focused on evaluating structure-property relationships in metallic powders, alloys, and composite specimens through advanced characterization techniques: X-ray Diffraction (XRD) for crystal indexing and lattice parameter determination, Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) coupled with quantitative line-intercept methods for grain size and phase distribution analysis, and Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) using selected area electron diffraction (SAED) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) to examine thermal annealing microstructures and quantify elemental mass ratios.

Laboratory Workflow & Technical Breakdown

The semester coursework followed a rigorous 3-stage characterization and quantitative analysis sequence:

01

1. X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) & Crystallography

Utilized a Rigaku MiniFlex 600 diffractometer with Cu-Kα radiation. Processed raw diffraction data and generated calibrated intensity vs. 2θ graphs using custom Python scripts (NumPy, Matplotlib), applying Bragg's Law and structure factor calculations to index peaks from 20° to 100° 2θ and fingerprint FCC Nickel, BCC Iron, and HCP Cobalt.

02

2. Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)

Evaluated topography and phase contrast for Cu, NiCo, and Ni-PTFE using an SU7000 SEM with Upper, PDBSE, and Lower detectors. Performed a quantitative 5-line intercept method on Copper PDBSE micrographs to calculate a mean grain size of 0.54 μm.

03

3. Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) & EDS

Investigated electrojet-polished Ni-Co thin foils annealed at 200–400°C using Bright Field (BF), Dark Field (DF), and Selected Area Electron Diffraction (SAED). Acquired Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDS) spectra to document microstructural evolution to ordered single-crystal grains at 400°C and applied Cliff-Lorimer quantification to determine alloy composition ratios.

PROJECT 04 — DETAILED REPORT

Chestnut Residence Mailroom Redevelopment

Project Management

Scope & Overview

This project delivered a comprehensive Conceptual Design Specification (CDS) to redevelop the underutilized Chestnut Student Residence mailroom into an accessible, multipurpose wellness space. The project established primary and secondary functions using black-box boundary modeling, mapped client and stakeholder metrics, generated 60 distinct design combinations via morphological analysis, and executed systematic feasibility, multi-voting, and Pugh Matrix decision matrix evaluations to select top conceptual designs.

Visual Design Workflow

The CDS followed a structured engineering design process from initial site investigation to final concept selection:

01
Stakeholder Map
cds_stakeholder.jpg

1. Problem Definition & Stakeholder Mapping

Defined project scope alongside primary client requirements (Residence Wellness Coordinator) and key stakeholders (Chestnut residents, maintenance staff, residence security). Established quantitative objectives for acoustic isolation, thermal regulation, and mood adjustable lighting.

02
Site Visit Data
cds_site_visit.jpg

2. Data Collection via Site Visit

Conducted an on-site audit of the Chestnut Residence mailroom to record spatial dimensions, existing structural obstructions, traffic bottlenecks, lighting levels, and ambient noise baselines. This primary data collection directly informed our constraints and acoustic attenuation targets.

03
Morph Chart
cds_morph_chart.jpg

3. Functional Analysis & Morphological Synthesis

Constructed a Functional Black Box diagram to isolate inputs and energy flows, establishing the primary function: transform underutilized mailroom space into a wellness environment. Built a comprehensive Morphological Chart spanning key secondary functions to generate 60 unique candidate design combinations.

04
Multi-Voting
cds_multivoting.jpg

4. Feasibility Screening & Multi-Voting Filter

Subjected all 60 generated concepts to strict physical, structural, and budget constraint checks based on site visit measurements. Applied a 2-stage team multi-voting protocol to filter candidates down to top-performing options for detailed evaluation.

05
Pugh Matrix
cds_pugh_matrix.jpg

5. Pugh Matrix Evaluation & Final Selection

Evaluated candidate designs against established objectives using a Pugh Decision Matrix with Datum comparisons, successfully narrowing down to 3 finalized alternate design options optimized for acoustic privacy and confidentiality.

The Engineering Process

From problem to solution

How I currently approach engineering problems

01⌕

Understand

Define the problem, requirements, constraints, and success criteria without assuming a solution.

02◇

Design & model

Develop concepts, CAD geometry, material choices, and assumptions based on research and knowledge.

03∿

Analyze

Use calculations, simulation, and engineering judgement to predict performance.

04⚙

Build & test

Manufacture a prototype or run tests where possible; record the evidence.

05↻

Iterate

Compare results against requirements and improve the design.

06✓

Validate

Summarize what worked, what remains uncertain, and the final outcome.
